# Rung 2 — The Inner Opposition ʃ ⊣ ♭

*Draft spec proposal (initial Phase 1 slice). Notation per `NOTATION.md`
(frozen); definitions D0.1–D0.4 and results 0.x/1.x per the preceding spec
files. Grades: **established** / **claimed** / **conjectural**; ⚙ marks
bounded computations in `examples/`. This file does not declare Phase 1 or
the full rung-2 exposition complete.*

**Summary of findings.** An adjoint triple
`Π₀ ⊣ Disc ⊣ Γ`, with `Disc` fully faithful, induces the inner-shape
adjoint modality `ʃ := Disc Π₀ ⊣ ♭ := Disc Γ`. Both moments are idempotent
and have exactly the discrete objects as fixed objects. If Π₀ preserves
finite products, so does ʃ. These data do **not exhibit a level** under
D0.2: the construction straddles two adjunctions rather than supplying one
essential-subtopos triple. Consequently D0.3–D0.4 do not yet type-check for
rung 2, and no level-1 Aufhebung claim is made. The canonical
`♭X → X → ʃX` is a unity; stronger fracture theorems require additional
hypotheses.

## 1. The inner-shape theorem

Let ℰ and 𝒮 be categories and suppose there is an adjoint triple

    Π₀ ⊣ Disc ⊣ Γ

with `Disc : 𝒮 → ℰ` fully faithful. Define endofunctors of ℰ

    ʃ := Disc ∘ Π₀,          ♭ := Disc ∘ Γ.

For the product statement below, suppose also that ℰ and 𝒮 have finite
products and Π₀ preserves them.

**Theorem 2.1 (inner shape).** There is an adjunction

    ʃ ⊣ ♭.

The functor ʃ is an idempotent monad and ♭ an idempotent comonad. Their
fixed objects coincide and are precisely the essential image of `Disc`.
The counit of `Disc ⊣ Γ` and the unit of `Π₀ ⊣ Disc` give the natural
unity

    ♭X → X → ʃX.

If Π₀ preserves finite products, then ʃ preserves finite products.
*Grade: established.*

*Proof.* Adjunction pasting gives natural bijections

    Homℰ(ʃX, Y)
      = Homℰ(Disc Π₀X, Y)
      ≅ Hom𝒮(Π₀X, ΓY)
      ≅ Homℰ(X, Disc ΓY)
      = Homℰ(X, ♭Y).

Since `Disc` is fully faithful, the counit `Π₀ Disc → id𝒮` of its
left adjunction and the unit `id𝒮 → Γ Disc` of its right adjunction are
isomorphisms. Hence

    ʃʃ ≅ Disc Π₀ Disc Π₀ ≅ Disc Π₀ = ʃ,
    ♭♭ ≅ Disc Γ Disc Γ ≅ Disc Γ = ♭.

An object is fixed by either composite exactly when it lies in the
essential image of the corresponding fully faithful `Disc`; therefore
the two fixed subcategories coincide. The displayed unity is the composite
of the constituent counit and unit. Finally, `Disc`, as a right adjoint to
Π₀, preserves finite products, so `Disc Π₀` does when Π₀ does. ∎

**Remark 2.2 (the source boundary).** Lawvere's 2007 definition of
cohesion assumes a four-adjoint string and additional conditions, including
finite-product preservation by Π₀ and preservation of parameterized
powers. Theorem 2.1 uses only the displayed triple, full faithfulness, and
the separately stated product hypothesis. It is not a converse
characterization of cohesion. *Grade: established (source-audited in
`notes/rung-1-to-2-research-dossier.md`).*

## 2. The Sierpiński topos

Let `𝒮p = Set^→`, with an object written `ξ : X₀ → X₁` as in the preceding
spec files. The discrete inclusion sends a set `S` to `(S = S)`.

**Proposition 2.3 (explicit formulas).**

    Π₀X = X₁,              ΓX = X₀,
    ʃX = (X₁ = X₁),        ♭X = (X₀ = X₀).

The unity is

    (X₀ = X₀) --(id,ξ)--> (X₀ → X₁) --(ξ,id)--> (X₁ = X₁).

Moreover Π₀ preserves finite products, and for all finite members X,Y of
the bounded test family,

    |Hom(ʃX,Y)| = |X₁ → Y₀| = |Hom(X,♭Y)|.

The fixed objects of both moments are exactly the arrows isomorphic to an
identity map. *Grade: established; bounded instances ⚙.*

*Proof.* The colimit of a single arrow is its codomain, so the left adjoint
to the diagonal is `Π₀X = X₁`. The global-sections formula `ΓX = X₀` was
proved in Theorem 1.1. Products of arrows are pointwise, whence
`Π₀(X × Y) = X₁ × Y₁ = Π₀X × Π₀Y`; the terminal object is equally
preserved. The formulas, unity, adjunction, and fixed-object statement now
follow from Theorem 2.1 (or directly). ∎

## 3. Simplicial sets

For a simplicial set X, let π₀X be its set of vertices modulo the
equivalence relation generated by the endpoints of 1-simplices. Let
`Disc S` be the constant simplicial set at S and `ΓX = X₀`.

**Proposition 2.4 (simplicial inner shape).**

    π₀ ⊣ Disc ⊣ Γ,
    ʃX = Disc(π₀X),        ♭X = Disc(X₀).

Both moments have the constant simplicial sets as their fixed objects, and
`π₀` preserves finite products. *Grade: established; finite examples
truncated in dimensions ≤ 2 are checked ⚙.*

*Proof.* A map from X to a constant simplicial set must assign the same
value to the two endpoints of every 1-simplex, hence factors uniquely
through π₀X. This proves `π₀ ⊣ Disc`; `Disc ⊣ Γ` is Theorem 1.1.

The canonical map `π₀(X × Y) → π₀X × π₀Y` is bijective. Surjectivity is
immediate on vertices. For injectivity, first traverse the X-coordinate's
finite edge-zigzag while holding the Y-coordinate fixed with degenerate
edges. Then traverse the Y-coordinate's zigzag while holding the
X-coordinate fixed with degenerate edges. This avoids any need to align
the orientations of edges in the two zigzags and produces a zigzag in
`X × Y`. The empty product is also preserved. Theorem 2.1 supplies the
remaining claims. ∎

**Bounded-computation boundary.** `examples/src/sset.mjs` represents only
finite truncations in dimensions 0, 1, and 2. Its π₀ computation uses
vertices and 1-simplices and checks named finite examples and their
products. It does not enumerate all simplicial sets and is not evidence for
the unbounded theorem beyond regression against these examples.

## 4. The D0.4 test: a typing defect, not an Aufhebung

**Proposition 2.5 (rung 2 is not thereby exhibited as a level).** The
inner-shape modality of Theorem 2.1 is not, merely from those hypotheses,
exhibited as a level in the sense of D0.2. Therefore expressions asserting
D0.3 order, D0.4 resolution, or an Aufhebung relation between level 1 and
rung 2 are not defined in the present spec. *Grade: established.*

*Proof.* A level is an **outer-shape** modality arising from a single
essential-subtopos triple `i₋ ⊣ i* ⊣ i₊`, whose two embeddings are the
outer functors. Here the fully faithful functor is instead the **middle**
functor `Disc` of `Π₀ ⊣ Disc ⊣ Γ`; D0.1 identifies the resulting
`ʃ ⊣ ♭` as inner shape. No essential subtopos exhibiting this pair as one
level has been supplied. Thus the inputs required by D0.3 and D0.4 are
absent. This does not rule out a separate essential-subtopos triple whose
moments happen to be equivalent in some model. ∎

**Finite witness in 𝒮p.** Proposition 2.3 gives
`ʃ = ◯_open` and `♭ = □_closed`. Proposition 1.7 proves that the open and
closed levels are incomparable. Thus the inner pair combines one moment
from each of two distinct levels; it is the opposition **across** the
diamond, not another vertex of it. ⚙

This result discharges `01-level-1.md` §7(b) negatively for the supplied
data: there is no D0.4 relation to calculate until a genuine candidate
level is specified. It does not prove that no other level deserves the
project name “level 2,” or that no model can exhibit the same pair by other
data; it proves that the inner-shape triple alone is insufficient.

## 5. Unity is not yet fracture

The natural composite `♭X → X → ʃX` exists under Theorem 2.1. No pullback,
fiber-sequence, or reconstruction claim follows from that theorem.

General modal fracture/gluing theorems require, for example, a lex modality
and a strong-disjointness hypothesis (Rijke–Shulman–Spitters, Theorem
3.50). Myers' cohesive modal fracture hexagon is stated for crisp
∞-groups, and Schreiber's differential-cohomology hexagon for spectrum
objects in cohesive homotopy theory. Those hypotheses are not present in
the two 1-topos computations here. *Grade: established as a source
boundary; a rung-2 fracture theorem for this project remains open.*
