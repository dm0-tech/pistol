## Statement

The slogan splits into: (1) **the language is interpreted from the topos
structure**—the Mitchell–Bénabou language uses finite limits, exponentials,
Ω, and their adjoint logical operations [theorem]; calling this
“secreted, not imported” and saying it fingerprints becoming are project
readings; (2) **truth is evaluated at generalized stages** in
Kripke–Joyal semantics [theorem]; comparing this with
observer-participancy is a reading, not the semantics theorem; (3)
**whether the book narrates its own binding**—whether Aufhebung is
internally statable—is exactly OP-3 [open].

Lawvere's diagonal theorem is a candidate obstruction only after a syntax
object, a typed truth predicate, and the relevant point-surjectivity
hypothesis are supplied. The untyped claim “no topos defines its own
truth” is not asserted. Further readings, all conjectural: Wheeler's
pregeometry may have run the arrow logic→world where topos semantics offers
world→logic; stratification or guarding may replace a total self-defining
loop; each rung's language may become able to *say* what the rung below
could only *do*.

## Context

The session's central clarification: the internal language is what toposes
add to bare adjoint structure (habitability), and the project is
"borderline theorizing a language that leaps off the page" in a sense that
is two-thirds theorem, one-third OP-3.

## What would change the labels

Claims 1–2 are source-audited at their narrowed mathematical scope. Claim 3
moves with OP-3. Any diagonal obstruction must first be typed; a successful
internal formulation or a counterexample changes that component. The
historical pregeometry reversal and observer-participancy readings require
their own source argument and should be attacked in the Phase 1 essay's red
leg.
