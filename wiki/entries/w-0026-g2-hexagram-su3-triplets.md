## Statement

*Established (machine-checked).*
- G₂ has 12 roots forming a hexagram: 6 short, 6 long, with length ratio
  √3.
- The Coxeter (Weyl) group is dihedral of order 12, generated by two
  reflections whose product has order h = 6. Hence 12 = rank·h roots and
  14 = rank·(h+1) dimensions.
- The long roots form a closed A₂ subsystem, the roots of SU(3) with Weyl
  group S₃. The short roots split into two W(A₂)-orbits of 3, the weights
  of 3 and 3̄. So 𝔤₂ = 𝔰𝔲(3) ⊕ 3 ⊕ 3̄, i.e. 14 = 8 + 6.

*Established (recalled, not computed here).* The weights of the
7-dimensional representation Im 𝕆 are the short roots plus 0, so
7 = 1 + 3 + 3̄ under SU(3). This matches the geometric SU(3) of W-0025:
fixing a unit (the 1) makes the remaining six directions ℂ³.

*Reading (conjectural, structural at most).* The Weyl group W = N(T)/T is
the residual symmetry of a maximal torus T: a finite, discrete shadow of G₂.
That is another "classical system in plain sight" (OP-24). The claim is a
structural resemblance only, and no functor to BGW's classical partners is
known.

*Pun guard.* This 12 is dihedral (hexagon symmetries). W-0009's 12 is
cyclic (π₆(S³) = ℤ/12). Do not connect them without a map.
