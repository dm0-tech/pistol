# An adjunction is an equivalence that failed, with the failure measured

## Statement

(i) An equivalence is precisely an adjunction whose unit and counit are
invertible; a general adjunction is therefore the structured record of a
failed equivalence — unit and counit localize the failure, the induced
(co)monad gives its shape, and the adjunction restricts to an equivalence
on the objects where unit/counit invert. (ii) Adjoints are unique up to
unique isomorphism. (iii) The concept is definable in the bare equational
language of 2-categories (the walking adjunction) and transfers to
∞-categories unchanged, via the homotopy 2-category. (iv) The kindergarten
modal example: ⌈−⌉ ⊣ ι ⊣ ⌊−⌋ for ℤ ↪ ℝ, an idempotent adjoint triple.
